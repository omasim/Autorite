## Beliefs retained and narrowed

Retain the contract principle that preservation and representation judgments require an explicit inquiry and comparison context. The examples now make several boundaries concrete: adjacent steps do not determine initial-reference excursion; current payload does not determine all genealogy answers; a restriction's effect on selected endpoints is separate from fixed-source graph inclusion.

These are scoped interpretations. History helps in the declared aliased predictive world, but history varying in E2 does not automatically help future prediction. R3's success does not prove it is the smallest encoding. UNKNOWN decoder responses do not all establish information loss. V1's supplied full-information rule does not establish learned-rule benefit, indefinite safety or a normative need for restrictions. Q-4's possible reduction to Q-3 remains unproved.

Foundation Review 1 retains PC01–03 FROZEN, PC04–06 DRAFT and the existing formal-capability contract. No recurring formal deficit or discriminating test among parked foundation candidates was established. Retaining the current baseline is an explicit bounded adequacy judgment, not evidence that it is final or optimal.
