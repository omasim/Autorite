## Query-relative information requirements

| Inquiry | Scoped assessment |
| --- | --- |
| State equality | Current states suffice for this exact comparison. Equality is not lineage evidence. |
| Strict ancestry | A complete parent DAG suffices; R0 G0/G3 and R1 G4/C4 show particular distinctions lost after projection. Equivalent reachability information could also answer the query. |
| Shared roots | Complete parent sets and root reachability suffice. A retained single-parent forest may lose a contributing root at merge. |
| Complete immediate parents | R2/R3 preserve them; G4/C4 show that a single pointer cannot recover the second parent without other information. |
| Birth operation | Untyped G1/G2 topology cannot distinguish copy from fork. Type labels or equivalent operation information are needed for those inquiries over a domain containing both cases. |
| Availability | Consumption matters: a survives copying but is consumed by fork; b survives C4 but is consumed by merge G4. Operation information suffices under this toy policy; an explicit availability record could serve this query instead. |

This is not a proof that a full R3 graph is the weakest sufficient representation. Query-specific summaries may retain fewer distinctions. Event chronology, fork grouping, ownership and contribution weights are absent from R3 and outside the audited inquiry family. Closure accepts explicit requirements and counterexamples, not a universal need for full history.

## Scientific limits and remaining questions

The corpus has six three-entity constructions, a common known universe, aligned opaque handles and equal states. It does not enumerate every admissible DAG. Parent selection in R1 is label-sensitive. Copy/fork consumption and two-input merges are declared synthetic rules. Calibration expectations were disclosed in advance; counts are deterministic checks, not empirical frequencies or confidence estimates. Prior-art inspection is targeted and external scientific review has not occurred.

Q-0 and Q-4 remain ACTIVE. Arbitrary graph sizes, changing states, multiway or repeated merges, richer lifecycle rules, chronology-sensitive inquiries, fork grouping, content contribution, uncertain provenance, minimal encodings and real-world continuation judgments remain open. Broader scope needs a new reviewed protocol. No broad Claim is promoted, no baseline definition is changed and no invented receiving Cycle is assigned unfinished work.
