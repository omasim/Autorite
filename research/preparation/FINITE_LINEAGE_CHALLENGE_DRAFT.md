# Finite lineage challenge — preparation draft

**Status: PREPARATION ONLY.** This is a working design note following D-0021. It does not open Cycle 03, create a package or Test, commit resources, freeze source or inputs, or authorize execution. The eight structural templates and the proposed state-equality sensitivity set below remain design proposals; no machine-readable candidate corpus has been generated and no candidate query outcomes have been computed. A separate synthetic software-only probe is recorded below; its answer outputs were not retained or inspected.

## Proposed question and boundary

For the two registered RP006 inquiry families, do the same eight whole-field masks over state (`S`), complete direct-parent sets (`P`), and birth operation (`K`) remain sufficient when the comparison corpus contains newly authored, valid lineage logs with four and five entities and both repeated and distinct state payloads?

This is a finite challenge to the transfer of RP006's classifications from six disclosed three-entity constructions to a separately specified corpus. A same-projection/different-answer pair falsifies sufficiency for any declared corpus containing that pair. No conflicts would establish only the finite classification of the reviewed corpus and mask/query class; it would not establish universal, probabilistic, natural-population, or bit-optimal sufficiency.

The registered queries remain unchanged: `same_state`, strict `ancestor`, `shared_root`, `parents`, `birth_kind`, and `alive` for `FULL`; the first four for `TOPOLOGY_STATE`. The eight masks remain `000` through `111` with the existing field meanings. Entity IDs are public and aligned within each stratum, as in RP006. Compare cases only within a stratum; four- and five-entity query universes are not pairwise aligned with one another.

### Decision value of the two inquiry families

The intended representation decision is conditional on two distinct uses. `FULL` represents an archive or audit view that must reproduce all six registered answers, including `birth_kind` and `alive`; any conflict rejects that mask for this use on the declared corpus. `TOPOLOGY_STATE` represents a narrower analysis that consumes only `same_state`, strict `ancestor`, `shared_root`, and `parents`; its value is to determine whether that use permits a smaller retained-field mask than the full audit use. A conflict rejects the mask for that narrower use, while no conflict supports only its finite classification on the declared cases. Because `TOPOLOGY_STATE` is a subset of `FULL`, retain both only if both uses are real and the narrower profile can change a field-retention decision. The entry reviewer must accept or remove this distinction before inputs or query outcomes exist; outcomes cannot select the query family set.

## Candidate finite scope

The proposed design has two entity-count strata (4 and 5) and four fixed structural templates in each. These eight templates form a purposive finite stress grid, not a sample or a minimal cover of either stratum. Their intended contrasts are: continuation beside a disconnected root; a branch following continuation; copy followed by a merge with an independent root; asymmetric development from two roots; unequal branch depth; a merge that leaves a branch sibling live; distinct state changes across independently rooted lines before merging; and a continuing line with a retained copy that merges with an independent root. These motifs target distinctions queried by `ancestor`, `shared_root`, `parents`, `birth_kind`, and `alive`; they do not establish that all relevant motifs are covered. The state-equality variants separately probe `same_state` and its interaction with masks. The templates must be checked against the six RP004 fixtures so overlap and any reused motifs are explicit. This is a proposed design, not an authored case inventory.

### Proposed template grid for structural review

The following event notation omits states: `root(x)`, `continue(x→y)`, `copy(x→y)`, `fork(x→y,z)`, and `merge(x,y→z)`. IDs are the public aligned IDs within each stratum. The sequences are proposals, not frozen event-log inputs. Their intended order is part of each template.

| Slot | Entities | Ordered event structure | Motif |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| F4-01 | 4 | `root(a); copy(a→b); continue(b→c); root(d)` | A copied lineage continues; a separate root supplies an unrelated component. |
| F4-02 | 4 | `root(a); continue(a→b); fork(b→c,d)` | A continued entity branches into two live leaves. |
| F4-03 | 4 | `root(a); copy(a→b); root(c); merge(b,c→d)` | A copy-derived entity merges with an independently rooted entity. |
| F4-04 | 4 | `root(a); root(b); copy(b→c); continue(a→d)` | Two roots develop differently, with a copy on one side and a continuation on the other. |
| F5-01 | 5 | `root(a); continue(a→b); fork(b→c,d); continue(c→e)` | A nested branch has unequal descendant depth. |
| F5-02 | 5 | `root(a); root(b); fork(a→c,d); merge(b,c→e)` | A branch is partly merged with an independent root; its other branch remains live. |
| F5-03 | 5 | `root(a); copy(a→b); root(c); continue(c→d); merge(b,d→e)` | Two independently rooted lines, with different operations, meet in a merge. |
| F5-04 | 5 | `root(a); root(b); continue(b→c); copy(c→d); merge(a,d→e)` | A two-step line meets a separate root, with a retained copy on the line. |

For a first legality pass, state vectors are listed in ID order and use opaque labels `A`, `B`, and `C`. These are proposed controls, not outcome-derived assignments. They obey the existing rule that `copy` and both `fork` outputs preserve the relevant parent's state; `continue` and `merge` may emit another opaque value.

| Slot | Proposed state vector (`a,b,c,d[,e]`) | Coverage role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F4-01 | `(A,A,B,A)` | Repetition, a changed continuation, and equal values on unrelated roots. |
| F4-02 | `(A,B,B,B)` | A changed continuation followed by state-preserving fork outputs. |
| F4-03 | `(A,A,B,B)` | Distinct values, with a merge output sharing the independent root's value. |
| F4-04 | `(A,A,A,A)` | Repeated values across unrelated roots. |
| F5-01 | `(A,B,B,B,C)` | A changed continuation and unequal branch depth, with fork state preserved. |
| F5-02 | `(A,B,A,A,B)` | Distinct values across roots, with the fork preserving `a` and the merge output assigned `B`. |
| F5-03 | `(A,A,B,C,A)` | Copy preservation and a changed continuation across distinct payloads, using at most three labels. |
| F5-04 | `(A,B,C,C,A)` | A changed continuation, copy preservation, and a merge output sharing `a`'s value. |

An in-memory, schema-only check using the existing RP004 construction grammar accepted all eight proposed event logs and their copy/fork state constraints. A separate ID-permutation comparison found no duplicate full event structures within either count stratum. The earlier prefix-only comparison was too narrow: candidates contain several parent-closed three-entity RP004 motifs, but parent closure alone does not make a fork-containing subset a replayable operation-closed sublog. The preliminary overlap audit below separates those criteria. Before entry, an independent reviewer should repeat the structural check and decide which overlap criterion is relevant to the transfer question.

The vectors above are illustrative members of the proposed state-equality sets, not the complete inputs. They give examples of repeated and distinct payloads, equal labels on unrelated roots, and changed values along connected lineages. They do not establish motif coverage or show that either inquiry family is sufficient for the research decision.

State assignment is a separate, predeclared factor. A global bijective renaming of opaque values cannot change equality-based `same_state` answers, so the sensitivity set varies equality partitions rather than label spellings. The proposed design enumerates every admissible partition using at most three opaque values for each fixed topology, subject to `copy` and `fork` preserving the parent's state. This produces 17 F4 and 38 F5 case variants. It is an exhaustive finite sensitivity set over these eight topologies and this three-value bound; it is not exhaustive over state spaces, lineage constructions, or natural populations. No variant may be selected or removed after outcomes are seen. Values may not encode a case, event position, answer, or intended conflict.

The deterministic enumeration rule is: form the forced-equality components induced by `copy` and `fork`; enumerate set partitions of those components into one, two, or three blocks; order blocks by first occurrence in entity-ID order; and enumerate the resulting restricted-growth strings lexicographically. Assign the canonical values `A`, `B`, and `C` to those ordered blocks, consistently across every case in both strata. The same symbol therefore denotes the same opaque value in all cases; no case may receive a separate arbitrary bijective relabeling before projection comparison. This is a fixed cross-case alignment convention, not a sensitivity analysis over all possible cross-case alignments. Case IDs combine the fixed topology slot with that stable partition ordinal. Producer inputs contain only the case ID and the event log with its assigned state payloads; topology/motif explanations and partition labels remain in review material, not as producer-side metadata. The independent reviewer must reproduce the enumeration and counts without computing query answers.

For the proposed sensitivity set, the eight topologies yield 17 admissible F4 cases and 38 admissible F5 cases (55 total). The expected work from the query inventory alone is 4,440 `FULL` truth rows, 440 projections (8 masks × 55 cases), 6,712 within-stratum case-pair/mask comparisons, and 32 family/mask certificates (2 query families × 8 masks × 2 strata). `TOPOLOGY_STATE` reuses the relevant subset of each case's truth rows. These are protocol arithmetic for a proposed design, not measured runtime, a resource commitment, or a run plan.

A second, locally written enumeration on 2026-10-14 independently generated restricted-growth strings over the entity positions (up to three blocks) and filtered only the copy/fork equality constraints. It reproduced per-slot counts `5, 2, 5, 5` and `5, 5, 14, 14`, then recomputed 55 cases, 4,440 `FULL` rows, 440 projections, 6,712 pair/mask comparisons, and 32 certificates. It did not construct event inputs, calculate answers, or inspect sufficiency/conflict outcomes. This is an internal cross-check using a different enumeration route, not the independent review required before entry; the external reviewer must still reproduce it.

## Construction and independence controls

- Reuse only the existing event grammar (`root`, `continue`, `copy`, `fork`, `merge`) if the entry review confirms that this is the intended domain. Every input must be a live prior entity; entity IDs must be fresh; operation arities and consumption rules must hold; the acyclic history is the ordered event log.
- Store only case identifiers and event logs in the candidate source. Do not store expected answers, mask verdicts, conflict labels, or per-case descriptive labels in producer input.
- Fix the structural slot definitions and state-payload assignment rule before materializing cases or inspecting their query outcomes. If generation uses a seed, commit the seed policy and generator before generating the final corpus; otherwise specify the deterministic enumeration and selection rule.
- Reconstruct truth directly from the event log in a verifier that does not import producer/query-generation/projection code. Independently enumerate the complete query inventory and check types as well as values.
- Preserve the equality-gated criterion: compare full answers only when canonical projections are exactly equal; record every differing aligned answer as a conflict witness. Retain all no-conflict, conflict, and incomparable minimal-mask outcomes.
- Include validation controls for invalid parent availability, duplicate/future parents, invalid event arity, copy/fork state violations, and an entity count outside the declared strata. Controls test implementation behavior; they do not become additional research cases.
- Compare the final candidate inventory structurally and by hash against the six old fixtures. Report overlap and the rule used to exclude simple extensions before entry review.

## Candidate input, verifier, and evidence contract

This section specifies a review target only. It does not create the candidate inputs, freeze a schema, or authorize a producer run.

### Input boundary

Use two separate future input documents: one for 17 four-entity cases and one for 38 five-entity cases. Each document should contain only a case identifier and its ordered event log, with state payloads attached to the corresponding event output under the accepted RP004 grammar. The public entity universe is `a`–`d` or `a`–`e`, respectively, and must be common to all cases in that document. Keeping strata as separate inputs makes cross-size comparison impossible by construction; a later evidence manifest may label the stratum but must not merge their comparison universes. Do not put expected query answers, mask results, conflict expectations, motif/coverage labels, or prose explanations in producer input. The illustrative vectors and all 55 variants remain review material until a separate input-freeze decision.

Before any future run, validate exact keys and types, unique case IDs, exact entity coverage, ordered event legality, and the copy/fork state-preservation rule. Reject unknown fields and malformed values rather than silently normalizing them. Hash the exact input bytes. Candidate identity and case ordering must be fixed before answer inspection; deterministic ordering should be by case ID, query family, mask, and canonical query key. The exact JSON canonicalization and byte-level serialization rules must be declared and versioned before freeze.

### Independent verifier boundary

The proposed verifier may rely on the accepted event semantics and replay domain, but it must independently reconstruct the entity facts and enumerate the registered query inventory. It must not import or call the future producer, its query enumerator, projection builder, conflict scanner, or certificate builder; it must not consume producer-generated truth rows as ground truth. The verifier should independently check input validity, truth rows, projected retained fields, all within-stratum pair/mask equality decisions, every equality-gated answer comparison, every conflict witness, certificate partitions and minimality claims, summary counts, and hashes. Producer and verifier should be authored and source-hashed separately, preferably by different people. They must not share a parser, domain-semantics implementation, query enumerator, projection builder, truth cache, conflict scanner or certificate logic. Use separate parsing and data reconstruction paths. If generic third-party runtime or serialization libraries are common to both, disclose them and verify that they contain no shared RP004/RP006 semantics. A disagreement is a failed verification, preserved with both outputs for diagnosis; it must not be resolved by editing expected answers or dropping a case after observing outcomes.

### Coverage arithmetic and edge handling

For the proposed 55-case inventory, the exact structural workload is:

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 4 entities × 17 cases | 1,020 | 136 | 1,088 | 16 |
| 5 entities × 38 cases | 3,420 | 304 | 5,624 | 16 |
| Total | 4,440 | 440 | 6,712 | 32 |

The per-case `FULL` query count is `3n² + 3n` (ordered pairs for the three pairwise families and one row per entity for the three unary families). `TOPOLOGY_STATE` reuses the truth rows for its registered first-four-family subset. The 1,088 and 5,624 comparison records are present even when projections differ; for each pair/mask record, projection equality is shared, but answer-comparison status and conflict/no-conflict findings are recorded separately for each inquiry family. Answer checks are performed only for equal projections, and their number is outcome-dependent. Pairs sharing one structural topology but differing in state partition remain valid within-stratum pairs and must be tagged as such in verifier-side summaries, not used to inflate the count of distinct topologies. No cross-stratum pairs are valid. These counts are protocol arithmetic, not query outcomes or measured work.

Each equal-projection comparison must retain all differing query answers, not just the first witness. An equal projection with no differing answer is recorded as an observed no-conflict pair; a non-equal projection is recorded as not answer-compared. Certificates must preserve the full equivalence partition for each mask/family, mark a partition's common answer vector only when all members agree, and record an explicit conflict state otherwise. Compute sufficiency and inclusion-minimality over all eight masks, retaining incomparable minimal masks. The protocol must not assume in advance that a conflict exists or that any particular mask is sufficient.

Coverage checks should independently assert: all 17 F4 and 38 F5 case IDs appear exactly once; every topology has exactly its full admissible equality-partition set; all eight masks occur per case; each expected family/query row occurs exactly once; every unordered pair in a stratum appears for every mask; all 32 certificate family/mask/stratum combinations exist; and the verifier rejects each invalid-input control listed above. Invalid-input controls are kept outside the research corpus and never counted in the table. Missing rows, duplicate rows, mismatched hashes, an omitted or duplicate partition, or a comparison across different strata invalidates the evidence package.

The verifier receipt is not itself proof of correctness; the verifier source and reproducible evidence remain part of the reviewable package. A future evidence contract must specify whether outputs are JSON or JSONL, canonical byte encoding, numeric/boolean typing, and stable row ordering before freeze. The manifest lists each finalized input and evidence file with its byte length and hash, explicitly excluding both the manifest itself and the later verifier receipt. Once the manifest is finalized, a separate receipt records its byte length and hash, hashes the exact evidence and verifier-source bytes, and binds the receipt to the source commit and input hashes. At present none of these output files exists for this challenge, and no candidate answers have been generated. The separate, non-research software feasibility probe below does not populate this evidence inventory.

**Serialization recommendation for entry review (not frozen):** use one UTF-8 `manifest.json`; JSONL files for truth, projection, pair comparison, and conflict witnesses; and JSON files for certificates, summary, and verifier receipt. Pair rows store a per-family conflict count and first witness ordinal rather than an unbounded ordinal array; certificate proper-submask evidence uses the same compact count/first-witness index. The conflict JSONL remains the complete source for every differing query and typed answer. This avoids repeating each witness ordinal up to 19 times across strict supermask certificates, while retaining a deterministic pointer to a witness. The 448-byte row limit applies only to the four JSONL files; `certificates.json` and every other non-row artifact count against the separate 64 MiB reserve. Require no BOM, LF line endings, a final LF, compact JSON, lexicographically sorted object keys, and no floating-point values. Preserve event order in inputs; sort case IDs and unordered pair endpoints lexicographically; order masks as `000`–`111`, query families by the registered family order, and query keys by their canonical typed JSON bytes. Encode answers with native JSON types (`true`/`false`, `null`, strings, and arrays of strings); never stringify booleans or nulls. Hash each exact input/evidence file byte sequence with lowercase SHA-256 hex and list its path, byte length, and hash in the manifest; explicitly omit `manifest.json` and the later verifier receipt. After finalizing the manifest, the external receipt hashes the manifest and evidence files and records the verifier-source hash and source/input identity. The entry reviewer should accept or amend this format before any input/source freeze; this proposal does not itself freeze serialization or create files.

## Prior-art boundary

View determinacy is already a formal information-theoretic property: equality of view outputs must force equality of query answers across the declared database-instance universe. The database-theory literature distinguishes finite-only from unrestricted instance universes, so this note must not use “finite determinacy” as a label for eight hand-selected cases. RP006 and the proposed challenge make finite-corpus comparisons; neither establishes determinacy over all finite databases ([Nash, Segoufin & Vianu, *Views and Queries: Determinacy and Rewriting*](https://dbucsd.github.io/paperpdfs/2010_3.pdf)).

A particularly close preservation result is Fan, Geerts and Zheng's formalization of selected-information preservation through invertibility and query preservation. They show that invertibility relative to a source query coincides with view determinacy, with results quantified over the declared database-instance class ([Fan et al., *View Determinacy for Preserving Selected Information in Data Transformations*, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.is.2011.09.001); [peer-reviewed author version](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/17752772/paper_viewd.pdf)). This makes query-relative preservation an established conceptual frame. The proposed work differs only at the operational level: a fixed finite corpus, fixed answer functions, and a declared whole-field mask class. That difference supports a reproducible finite challenge, not a new determinacy concept or a novelty claim.

The graph-view collision is direct. Work on path-query views studies determinacy over graph databases, and recent work studies regular-path views under walk-based semantics. Those papers vary query languages and quantify over database graphs; they are not this fixed aligned-entity corpus or its eight whole-field masks. They rule out presenting ancestry-query sufficiency as a new general determinacy concept ([Francis, *Asymptotic Determinacy of Path Queries using Union-of-Paths Views*](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICDT.2015.44); [Francis, *View-Based Query Determinacy for Walk-Based Semantics*](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICDT.2026.22)).

Following references from the graph-view literature also surfaces foundational overlaps. *Answering Queries Using Views* studies complete and minimal rewritings from materialized views; *Lossless Regular Views* asks when graph/path views preserve enough information to answer a query; and Afrati studies the relationship between determinacy and rewriting for conjunctive-query views. Together these make query-relative preservation and recovery from reduced information an established problem family, even though their language-defined views and database-wide semantics differ from the proposed finite, aligned-case comparison ([Levy et al., PODS 1995](https://doi.org/10.1145/212433.220198); [Calvanese et al., PODS 2002](https://iris.uniroma1.it/handle/11573/252669); [Afrati, *Determinacy and Query Rewriting for Conjunctive Queries and Views*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2010.12.031)).

Provenance work also studies abstraction and information loss. Existing systems summarize why/why-not provenance by grouping derivation patterns; SGProv compresses multiple workflow provenance graphs while retaining selected query answers; and later provenance-graph work segments derivations and summarizes similar paths under provenance constraints. These overlap with the concern that reduced fields may collapse distinctions relevant to answers; they use different representations, query tasks, and summaries ([Lee et al., *Integrating Approximate Summarization with Provenance Capture*](https://www.usenix.org/conference/tapp17/workshop-program/presentation/lee); [El-Jaick et al., *SGProv: Summarization Mechanism for Multiple Provenance Graphs*](https://periodicos.ufmg.br/index.php/jidm/article/view/249); [Miao & Deshpande, *Understanding Data Science Lifecycle Provenance via Graph Segmentation and Summarization*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04599)). Database provenance also has established algebraic lineage models ([Green, Karvounarakis & Tannen, *Provenance Semirings*](https://web.cs.ucdavis.edu/~green/papers/pods07.pdf)), and W3C PROV explicitly models derivation and qualified derivation ([PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/#term-Derivation), [PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/#qualifiedDerivation)).

The additional graph-summarization search found a closer answer-preservation family. Fan et al.'s SIGMOD work compresses a graph relative to a declared class of reachability or graph-pattern queries and preserves their answers through query transformation; Niazmand et al.'s query-based RDF summaries likewise target lossless retrieval and complete SPARQL answers. These are strong adjacent precedents for preserving answers after information reduction. Their objects are compressed/summary graphs and their guarantee is tied to a query class and transformed query execution. This draft instead compares fixed whole-field projections over a predeclared finite, aligned event-log corpus and checks registered answer functions directly; that operational distinction does not establish novelty ([Fan et al., *Query Preserving Graph Compression*, SIGMOD 2012](https://doi.org/10.1145/2213836.2213855); [peer-reviewed author copy](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/query-preserving-graph-compression/); [Niazmand et al., *Efficient Semantic Summary Graphs for Querying Large Knowledge Graphs*, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jjimei.2022.100082)). Other graph-summary work explicitly optimizes query-answer accuracy under lossy structural summaries, which is adjacent but uses probabilistic summaries and graph statistics rather than lineage-field masks ([LeFevre & Terzi, *GraSS: Graph Structure Summarization*, SDM 2010](https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9781611972801.40)).
